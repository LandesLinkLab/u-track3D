MLE Diffusion Analysis: Translational & Rotational

Based on Bo Shuang (Langmuir 2013) and Berglund (Phys. Rev. E 2010)

MLE 1: Known Localization Precision (Eq. 3-4)

Translational MLE 1 Spatial

Log-likelihood:

\[ L(\Delta s) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \log \left\{ \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi D_s n_i \Delta t}} \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta s_i^2}{4 D_s n_i \Delta t}\right) \right\} \]

Closed-form estimate:

\[ D_s^{(1)} = \frac{1}{2N\Delta t} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{\Delta s_i^2}{n_i} \]

Rotational MLE 1 Angular

Log-likelihood:

\[ L(\Delta \alpha) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \log \left\{ \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi D_\alpha n_i \Delta t}} \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta \alpha_i^2}{4 D_\alpha n_i \Delta t}\right) \right\} \]

Closed-form estimate:

\[ D_\alpha^{(1)} = \frac{1}{2N\Delta t} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{\Delta \alpha_i^2}{n_i} \]

MLE 2: Joint Estimation with Motion Blur (Eq. 5-8)

Translational Covariances (Eq. 5) Spatial

\[ \langle \Delta s_k \Delta s_k \rangle = 2D_s n_k \Delta t - 2(2D_s R\Delta t - \sigma_s^2) = \alpha_k \] \[ \langle \Delta s_{k \pm 1} \Delta s_k \rangle = 2D_s R\Delta t - \sigma_s^2 = \beta \] \[ \langle \Delta s_k \Delta s_j \rangle = 0 \quad \text{otherwise} \]

Rotational Covariances (Eq. 5 analog) Angular

\[ \langle \Delta \alpha_k \Delta \alpha_k \rangle = 2D_\alpha n_k \Delta t - 2(2D_\alpha R\Delta t - \sigma_\alpha^2) = \alpha_k \] \[ \langle \Delta \alpha_{k \pm 1} \Delta \alpha_k \rangle = 2D_\alpha R\Delta t - \sigma_\alpha^2 = \beta \] \[ \langle \Delta \alpha_k \Delta \alpha_j \rangle = 0 \quad \text{otherwise} \]

Log-likelihood (Eq. 7) Spatial Angular

\[ L(\Delta) = -\frac{1}{2} \log|\Sigma| - \frac{1}{2} \Delta^T \Sigma^{-1} \Delta \]

Same form for both translational (Δs) and rotational (Δα) diffusion.

Translational Covariance Matrix (Eq. 8) Spatial

\[ (\Sigma_s)_{ij} = [2D_s n_i \Delta t - 2(2D_s R\Delta t - \sigma_s^2)]\delta_{i,j} + [2D_s R\Delta t - \sigma_s^2]\delta_{i,j\pm 1} \]

Simplified:

\[ \alpha_i = 2D_s \Delta t (n_i - 2R) + 2\sigma_s^2 \quad \text{(diagonal)} \] \[ \beta = 2D_s R\Delta t - \sigma_s^2 \quad \text{(off-diagonal)} \]

Rotational Covariance Matrix (Eq. 8 analog) Angular

\[ (\Sigma_\alpha)_{ij} = [2D_\alpha n_i \Delta t - 2(2D_\alpha R\Delta t - \sigma_\alpha^2)]\delta_{i,j} + [2D_\alpha R\Delta t - \sigma_\alpha^2]\delta_{i,j\pm 1} \]

Simplified:

\[ \alpha_i = 2D_\alpha \Delta t (n_i - 2R) + 2\sigma_\alpha^2 \quad \text{(diagonal)} \] \[ \beta = 2D_\alpha R\Delta t - \sigma_\alpha^2 \quad \text{(off-diagonal)} \]

3D Translational: Sum Over Dimensions Spatial

\[ L_{\text{total}} = L(\Delta x) + L(\Delta y) + L(\Delta z) \]

Apply MLE2 separately to each spatial dimension and sum the log-likelihoods.

Definitions & Notation

Variable Definitions

Translational Rotational Meaning Units
\(\Delta s_i\) \(\Delta \alpha_i\) Displacement for step \(i\) nm / rad
\(D_s\) \(D_\alpha\) Diffusion coefficient nm²/s / rad²/s
\(\sigma_s\) \(\sigma_\alpha\) Localization/orientation precision nm / rad
\(R\) Motion blur coefficient dimensionless
\(n_i\) Frame gap for step \(i\) frames
\(\Delta t\) Frame time seconds

Kronecker Delta (δ)

\[ \delta_{i,j} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } i = j \\ 0 & \text{if } i \neq j \end{cases} \]

\(\delta_{i,j}\) selects diagonal elements; \(\delta_{i,j\pm 1}\) selects adjacent off-diagonal elements.

Motion Blur Coefficient R (Berglund Eq. 10)

\[ R = \frac{1}{\Delta t} \int_0^{\Delta t} S(t)[1 - S(t)] \, dt \]

Where \(S(t)\) is the cumulative shutter function (fraction of illumination before time \(t\)).

Illumination Type R Value Description
Stroboscopic/Pulsed \(R = 0\) No motion blur (instantaneous exposure)
Continuous/Uniform \(R = 1/6\) Most common experimental case
Double-pulse \(R = 1/4\) Maximum motion blur

Angular Displacement Calculation

Angular Distance Between Orientations Angular

\[ \Delta \alpha = \arccos(|\hat{\mu}_1 \cdot \hat{\mu}_2|) \]

Where the unit orientation vector is:

\[ \hat{\mu} = (\sin\theta \cos\phi, \, \sin\theta \sin\phi, \, \cos\theta) \]

And the dot product expands to:

\[ \hat{\mu}_1 \cdot \hat{\mu}_2 = \sin\theta_1 \sin\theta_2 \cos(\phi_1 - \phi_2) + \cos\theta_1 \cos\theta_2 \]

The absolute value \(|\cdot|\) accounts for head-tail symmetry of the dipole (orientations θ,φ and π-θ,φ+π are equivalent). Range: \(0 \leq \Delta\alpha \leq \pi/2\).

⚠️ Rotational MLE Approximation Note

The rotational MLE formulation assumes angular displacements are Gaussian-distributed, which is an approximation. Key limitations:

  • Small angle approximation: Valid when \(D_\alpha \cdot \Delta t \ll 1\) rad². For fast rotational diffusion or long time lags, the Gaussian assumption breaks down.
  • Bounded distribution: Angular distance \(\Delta\alpha\) is bounded to \([0, \pi/2]\) due to head-tail symmetry, unlike true Gaussian distributions which extend to ±∞.
  • Spherical geometry: Rotational diffusion occurs on a sphere (SO(3) or S²), not in Euclidean space. Rigorous treatment would use wrapped distributions or spherical harmonics.

More rigorous alternatives:

  • Orientation correlation functions: \(\langle P_2(\cos \Delta\alpha) \rangle = e^{-6D_\alpha t}\) (second Legendre polynomial)
  • Perrin equations for anisotropic rotational diffusion
  • Proper wrapped distributions on the rotation group

For typical single-molecule experiments with \(D_\alpha \sim 0.01-0.1\) rad²/s and \(\Delta t \sim 50\) ms, the Gaussian approximation is reasonable (\(D_\alpha \cdot \Delta t \sim 0.0005-0.005\) rad²).

Physical Meaning of Off-Diagonal Terms

Why β = 2DRΔt - σ²?

The off-diagonal term captures two competing effects:

  • Motion blur correlation (+2DRΔt): During exposure, the particle's position is time-averaged, creating positive correlation between adjacent measurements.
  • Localization error anti-correlation (-σ²): Adjacent displacements share a common endpoint. If position \(x_i\) has error \(+\epsilon\):
    • \(\Delta_{i-1} = x_i - x_{i-1}\) is biased by \(+\epsilon\)
    • \(\Delta_i = x_{i+1} - x_i\) is biased by \(-\epsilon\)
    This creates negative covariance.

When \(R = 0\) (no motion blur): \(\beta = -\sigma^2\), recovering the simpler case where only localization error causes correlation.
